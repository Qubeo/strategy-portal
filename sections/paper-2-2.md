A Programmable Organizational Domain is not a product — it is an institutional primitive. A POD enables any entity to establish a sovereign digital presence, manage its data and economic participation, and connect to a commons-based ecosystem — all governed by protocols rather than platforms.

The core value proposition: *"Have a digital twin of your organization. We provide secure data, local AI."* But this framing, while useful for adoption, understates the structural significance. PODs represent a new category of institutional form — modular, composable, and governed by their participants.

This typology is not arbitrary — it maps the full spectrum of organizational life in a bioregion, from the individual citizen to the bioregional whole. Each POD type instantiates the Self/Together/Others logic at its own scale, with its own governance protocols and data boundaries. The nesting pattern — citizens within families, families within municipalities, municipalities within bioregions — mirrors Ostrom's principle of nested governance for commons management.
