Elinor Ostrom's Nobel Prize-winning research demonstrated that communities can sustainably govern shared resources when institutional arrangements match the resource's characteristics. Her eight design principles — from clearly defined boundaries to nested enterprises — provide the institutional blueprint for digital commons governance.

Applied to the regenerative digital ecosystem: POD boundaries define who governs what; collective-choice arrangements are encoded in protocols; monitoring happens through verifiable credentials; graduated sanctions and conflict resolution are built into smart contract logic; and nested governance mirrors the POD typology from citizen to bioregion.
