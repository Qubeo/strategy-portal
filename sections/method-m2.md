Igor Calzada's research, spanning from "Smart City Citizenship" (2021) through his recent work on data cooperatives and algorithmic nations, provides a multi-layered analytical framework for understanding digital sovereignty:

- **Institutional sovereignty** — embedded in public agencies, hospitals, municipalities. The capacity of existing institutions to govern their own digital infrastructure.
- **Cooperative sovereignty** — realized through federated data cooperatives and trusts. New institutional forms enabling collective data governance.
- **Geopolitical sovereignty** — enacted through interregional alliances and AI standards. The macro-level context shaping what local sovereignty is possible.

Calzada's concept of "Algorithmic Nations" — drawing on Arendtian political theory — demonstrates how communities can attain self-determination through data sovereignty, offering a post-Westphalian frame particularly relevant for bioregional governance.
