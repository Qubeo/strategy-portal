## 2. Purpose & Positioning

### What this document IS:
- A **paradigmatic claim** — staking a position in the European discourse on digital sovereignty, grounding it in regenerative economics and commons governance
- A **strategic framework** — offering a replicable model for how Digital Innovation Hubs can catalyze bioregional digital ecosystems
- A **demonstration artifact** — the document itself embodies the principles it advocates (openness, layered access, evolving commons)
- A **coordination device** — enabling diverse stakeholders to find their entry point and see themselves in the vision

### What this document is NOT:
- A product whitepaper or platform pitch
- A technical architecture document
- A grant application or project report
- A blockchain/crypto manifesto

### Core Thesis (one paragraph):
Digital sovereignty, when pursued in isolation, produces fragmentation. When embedded within a regenerative economic paradigm and enacted through commons-based institutional innovation, it becomes the foundation for a fundamentally different kind of digital ecosystem — one that builds collective capability rather than extracting individual value. The DigiHub Liechtenstein, operating within the EDIH network, is prototyping this approach at bioregional scale, demonstrating that small, agile jurisdictions can serve as laboratories for institutional patterns that are globally replicable.

## 7. Key Vocabulary to Define / Use Consistently

| Term | Working Definition |
|------|-------------------|
| Regenerative Digital Ecosystem | A bioregional digital infrastructure and institutional arrangement designed to build collective capability, governed as commons, operating within ecological and social boundaries |
| Digital Self-Determination | The capacity of individuals, organizations, and communities to make meaningful choices about their digital infrastructure, data, and economic participation — framed as increasing autonomy rather than absolute independence |
| Programmable Organizational Domain (POD) | A modular, sovereign digital-institutional unit that enables an entity (citizen, family, cooperative, municipality, etc.) to manage its digital presence, data, and economic participation within a commons-based ecosystem |
| Commons Domain | A shared resource area (energy, data, logistics) requiring collective governance and coordination, where individual action is insufficient but collective action creates surplus value |
| Sovereignty Flywheel | The self-reinforcing cycle through which organizations achieving digital self-determination generate knowledge and infrastructure that makes subsequent transformations easier and cheaper |
| Commons Dividend | The accumulated shared capability that results from each participant's sovereignty journey — templates, frameworks, expertise, infrastructure — that reduce costs for subsequent participants |
| Self / Together / Others | Strategic framework for categorizing digital capabilities: what must be controlled directly (Self), what should be built collectively (Together), and what can be safely sourced from external providers (Others) |
| Civic Protocol Economy | An economic coordination pattern combining civic economy principles (relational, wellbeing-oriented) with protocol-based infrastructure (smart contracts, DIDs, automated governance) |
