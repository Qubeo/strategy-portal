Wardley Mapping, developed by Simon Wardley, plots value chains across an axis of evolution — from genesis through custom-built and product to commodity/utility. It reveals how components naturally evolve through supply and demand competition, making dependencies, leverage points, and strategic options visible.

Wardley's core argument about digital sovereignty is that most sovereignty conversations happen without maps — they are storytelling exercises disconnected from the actual landscape. His "Sovereignty and Landscape" analysis argues that sovereignty, like physical territory, requires cartographic understanding before strategic decisions can be made.

In this paper, Wardley Mapping is applied to: map the digital dependency landscape of a bioregional entity; identify which components belong in Self, Together, or Others categories; and anticipate how the landscape is evolving, where new leverage points are emerging.
