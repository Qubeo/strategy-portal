Regenerative
Digital Ecosystems

A strategic position for bioregional digital sovereignty — from extraction to circulation, from dependency to collective capability.

DigiHub Liechtenstein · digihub.li · EDIH Network · March 2026
In One Minute

Digital sovereignty, when pursued in isolation, produces fragmentation. When embedded within a regenerative economic paradigm and enacted through commons-based institutional innovation, it becomes the foundation for a fundamentally different kind of digital ecosystem — one that builds collective capability rather than extracting individual value. The DigiHub Liechtenstein, operating within the European Digital Innovation Hub network, is prototyping this approach at bioregional scale. Through Programmable Organizational Domains, commons-based infrastructure, and collaborative finance mechanisms, it demonstrates that small, agile jurisdictions can serve as laboratories for institutional patterns that are globally replicable. The question is not whether to pursue digital sovereignty, but what kind of economy sovereignty serves.

Approach

This position paper draws on multiple analytical frameworks, each illuminating different aspects of the regenerative digital ecosystem. We make our methods explicit because transparency in reasoning is itself a commons value. The sensemaking process — design sprints, Wardley mapping workshops, stakeholder co-creation — is not merely how the document was produced; it is part of what the document advocates.

Glossary

Shared Vocabulary

Regenerative Digital Ecosystem A bioregional digital infrastructure and institutional arrangement designed to build collective capability, governed as commons, operating within ecological and social boundaries.
Digital Self-Determination The capacity of individuals, organizations, and communities to make meaningful choices about their digital infrastructure, data, and economic participation — increasing autonomy rather than absolute independence.
POD Programmable Organizational Domain — a modular, sovereign digital-institutional unit enabling entities to manage their digital presence, data, and economic participation within a commons-based ecosystem.
Commons Domain A shared resource area (energy, data, logistics) requiring collective governance, where individual action is insufficient but collective action creates surplus value.
Sovereignty Flywheel The self-reinforcing cycle where achieving digital self-determination generates knowledge and infrastructure that makes subsequent transformations easier and cheaper.
Commons Dividend The accumulated shared capability from each participant's sovereignty journey — templates, expertise, infrastructure — that reduce costs for subsequent participants.
Self / Together / Others Framework for categorizing digital capabilities: what must be controlled directly, what should be built collectively, and what can be safely sourced externally.
Civic Protocol Economy Economic coordination combining civic economy principles (relational, wellbeing-oriented) with protocol-based infrastructure (smart contracts, DIDs, automated governance).
